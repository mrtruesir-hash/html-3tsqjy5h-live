18+ only

Gambling products discussed on this site are intended for adults of legal gambling age in their jurisdiction (18 or over in most places, though the exact minimum age depends on local law). If you are under the applicable legal age, please do not gamble.

Gambling involves financial risk

Betting and casino games are forms of entertainment with a real chance of losing money — there is no strategy or system that guarantees a win. Never gamble with money you need for essential expenses, and never gamble to try to recover previous losses ("chasing losses").

Principles of responsible play

  • Set a budget before you start, and stick to it regardless of whether you're winning or losing.
  • Set a time limit for play, and take regular breaks.
  • Treat gambling as entertainment with a cost, not as a way to make money or solve financial problems.
  • Never gamble under the influence of alcohol or when making decisions you wouldn't make with a clear head.
  • Don't gamble as a way to cope with stress, loneliness, or a bad day.
  • Keep gambling separate from money needed for bills, rent, or family expenses.

Warning signs of problem gambling

Consider stepping back and seeking support if you notice any of the following, in yourself or someone you know:

  • Spending more time or money on gambling than you intended to.
  • Chasing losses by betting more to try to win back what's already been lost.
  • Borrowing money, selling belongings, or missing bill payments to fund gambling.
  • Lying to family or friends about how much time or money is spent gambling.
  • Feeling irritable, anxious, or unable to stop when trying to cut back or quit.
  • Gambling affecting work, studies, relationships, or sleep.
  • Feeling that gambling is no longer fun, but continuing anyway.

Where to get help

If gambling has stopped being fun or is affecting your life, real, free support is available. Two internationally-facing organizations that support people affected by problem gambling, wherever you're based, are:

  • Gamblers Anonymous — a peer-support fellowship with meetings and resources in many countries.
  • Gambling Therapy — free support and advice for people affected by problem gambling, available in multiple languages.

If a national or local support service exists where you live, it may be able to offer more specific help than an international resource — search for gambling-support services in your own country, or ask a doctor or local health service for a referral.
