What Is an iGaming Affiliate Program?

By Ray Truelead, Head of Affiliates · Published 2026-08-19 · Updated 2026-08-19

An iGaming affiliate program pays you for referring real-money players to a sportsbook, casino or other gambling operator. You do not build the product or hold player funds — you send traffic, the operator converts it into paying players, and you earn a share of that value.

The three parties in every deal

There are always three parties: the operator (the licensed sportsbook or casino brand), the affiliate (you — a publisher, media buyer or content creator), and the player (the person who registers and deposits through your link). The operator pays the affiliate; the affiliate never charges the player directly.

This structure is what separates iGaming affiliate marketing from selling a physical product. You are not selling anything to the player. You are pointing them at an offer they were already interested in, and the operator pays you for the introduction because acquiring players any other way (ads, sponsorships) usually costs more.

How it differs from general affiliate marketing

Retail affiliate marketing (Amazon Associates, SaaS referral programs) typically pays a one-time commission on a purchase. iGaming affiliate programs are built around ongoing player value: a single referred player might generate revenue for months or years, and several commission structures exist specifically to let you capture that ongoing value rather than a single payout.

iGaming affiliate marketing also carries real regulatory weight that most retail affiliate niches don't: age verification, licensing jurisdiction, responsible-gambling messaging and GEO restrictions all matter in a way they simply don't for, say, a kitchen-gadget review site. What commission models exist

Three models cover almost every deal in the industry: CPA (a fixed payment per qualifying player), RevShare (an ongoing percentage of what the player generates), and Hybrid (both, combined). We cover the mechanics and the maths behind each in a dedicated comparison: CPA vs RevShare vs Hybrid.

What an affiliate actually does day to day

  • Builds or buys traffic (a site, a Telegram channel, paid ads, a social audience) around betting, casino or a related interest.
  • Places a unique tracking link or code so the operator's system can attribute new signups to that traffic.
  • Monitors a reporting dashboard for clicks, registrations, first deposits (FTDs) and earnings.
  • Optimises whatever is converting poorly — a landing page, a GEO, a traffic source — and requests payout once thresholds are met.

A simple example

Example: a sports-content Telegram channel with 5,000 members places a tracking link in its pinned message. Over a month, 400 people click it, 60 register with the operator, and 18 make a qualifying first deposit. Those 18 players are now the affiliate's referred players for commission purposes, regardless of which model the deal uses.

FAQ

Do I need a license to run an iGaming affiliate site?

Generally no — the operator holds the gambling license, not the affiliate. However, some jurisdictions do regulate gambling advertising and affiliate marketing specifically, so check the rules that apply to your own location and the GEOs you target.

Is iGaming affiliate marketing the same as being a bookmaker?

No. An affiliate never accepts bets, holds player funds, or operates the gambling product. You only refer traffic; the licensed operator runs the actual sportsbook or casino.

How is this different from promoting a betting tipster service?

A tipster sells predictions directly to customers. An iGaming affiliate earns from the operator, not the player, based on the value of the players referred — there's no product being sold to your audience.
