iGaming Affiliate Marketing: Complete Guide

By Ray Truelead, Head of Affiliates · Published 2026-08-19 · Updated 2026-08-19

iGaming affiliate marketing is the business of referring real-money sportsbook and casino players to a licensed operator in exchange for commission. This guide brings together everything a new or intermediate affiliate needs to actually evaluate and run a program, with links to a deeper, focused guide on each specific topic below.

What iGaming affiliate marketing is

You (the affiliate) send traffic — visitors from a site, a Telegram channel, paid ads or a social audience — to a licensed sportsbook or casino operator using a unique tracking link. The operator pays you based on the players that traffic converts into, not on the traffic itself. See What Is an iGaming Affiliate Program? for the full breakdown of who's involved and how it differs from ordinary affiliate marketing.

How an iGaming affiliate program works

Mechanically: a click is tracked and attributed to you → the visitor registers → they make a qualifying first deposit (FTD) → they continue (or don't) playing, generating revenue you may keep earning from depending on your commission model. Our full walkthrough of this funnel covers each stage with a worked example.

Affiliate tracking

Attribution relies on either browser cookies or server-to-server (postback) tracking, within a defined attribution window. Tracking quality directly determines whether you get paid for traffic you legitimately sent — see iGaming Affiliate Tracking for how each method works and the common ways tracking silently breaks.

CPA

Cost Per Acquisition pays a fixed amount the moment a referred player meets a qualifying condition, usually a minimum first deposit. It's fast and predictable but caps your upside on any single player regardless of how long they keep playing afterward.

RevShare

Revenue Share pays you an ongoing percentage of the net revenue a referred player generates, for as long as they stay active — potentially months or years from a single referral. See What Is RevShare? for how net revenue is actually calculated and a worked monthly-income example.

Hybrid commissions

Hybrid combines a smaller upfront CPA with a reduced ongoing RevShare on the same player, hedging between fast cash flow and long-term upside. Our CPA vs RevShare vs Hybrid comparison includes the actual break-even formula for deciding between all three, or compare your own numbers directly with our CPA vs RevShare Calculator.

Registration vs FTD

A registration is simply an account created; a First Time Deposit (FTD) is the first qualifying real-money deposit that account makes. The gap between the two — some registered players never deposit — is one of the most important conversion rates to monitor, because most CPA commissions trigger on FTD, not registration.

Conversion rates

Three funnel rates matter: click-to-registration, registration-to-FTD, and (for RevShare) ongoing player retention. Each isolates a different part of the funnel — traffic quality, the operator's signup flow, and the operator's product/retention respectively. See iGaming Affiliate KPIs for the exact formulas and an EPC worked example.

Revenue calculation

Example calculation: 2,000 clicks convert at 6% to 120 registrations, and 30% of those (36 players) make a qualifying FTD. Under a hypothetical $90 CPA, that's 36 × $90 = $3,240 once those players are confirmed as qualifying. Under a hypothetical 40% RevShare with an average $45 net revenue per player that month, the same 36 players generate roughly 36 × $45 × 0.40 = $648 that month alone — and again the following month if they remain active. Neither number is a guarantee; they illustrate how the same funnel produces very different cash-flow shapes under each model. Run your own numbers with our free Affiliate Revenue Calculator.

Traffic sources

Common sources include content/SEO sites, Telegram and social channels, paid media, tipster communities and review sites. Different sources suit different commission models: paid traffic with high churn often favours CPA, while loyal organic audiences tend to reward RevShare over time.

Content marketing

Reviews, comparisons, how-to guides and market-specific content (like this guide series) build the kind of trust that produces higher-quality, longer-retained referrals than pure paid acquisition — which is precisely why RevShare rewards it more than CPA does.

SEO for iGaming affiliates

iGaming is a competitive, high-value niche, so genuine information gain (real comparisons, real numbers, honest limitations) tends to outperform templated affiliate content. Structured data, clear E-E-A-T signals (a real named author, disclosed affiliate relationships) and topical depth — the approach this guide series itself follows — are what search engines and readers both reward here.

Affiliate compliance

Gambling advertising is regulated in many jurisdictions: age-gating (18+), responsible-gambling messaging, and restrictions on specific claims or GEOs are common requirements, not optional extras. Confirm the rules for your own location and every GEO you target — see our own Responsible Gambling and Affiliate Disclosure pages for the standard we hold this site to.

GEO considerations

Player value, competition and regulation all vary enormously by country. A program's broad regional claims ("Africa, Asia, CIS...") only matter if they cover your specific target countries — always confirm eligibility for your exact GEO rather than assuming a regional description applies.

Payment models

Beyond commission structure, check the actual payout mechanics: schedule (weekly/monthly), minimum thresholds, and supported payment methods (bank transfer, e-wallets, crypto). These operational details affect your cash flow as much as the headline commission rate does.

How to evaluate an affiliate program

Our dedicated How to Choose an iGaming Affiliate Program guide is a seven-point checklist covering commission flexibility, published terms, payout reliability, tracking, GEO relevance, support responsiveness and operator licensing — everything above, turned into a comparison framework.

Common affiliate mistakes

  • Chasing the highest headline commission percentage without checking real EPC or break-even numbers for your own traffic.
  • Ignoring tracking reliability until a suspiciously low conversion rate forces the question.
  • Sending traffic to GEOs or with methods that were never actually confirmed as eligible.
  • Treating a single program's numbers as representative of the whole industry, rather than comparing several with real data.
  • Skipping compliance basics (age-gating, responsible-gambling messaging) that protect both the audience and the affiliate's own standing with the program.

FAQ

Do I need a gambling license to be an iGaming affiliate?

No — the licensed operator holds the gambling license, not the affiliate. You should still check whether your own location or target GEOs regulate gambling advertising specifically.

Which commission model earns the most?

It depends entirely on how long your referred players stay active. Run the break-even calculation in our CPA vs RevShare guide with your own retention data rather than assuming either model wins by default.

How much traffic do I need to start?

There's no universal minimum — many programs accept small, genuine traffic sources. Focus on legitimate, relevant traffic and a clear promotion plan rather than chasing a specific volume threshold before applying.

Is iGaming affiliate marketing legal?

Affiliate marketing itself is generally legal, but promoting real-money gambling is subject to local advertising and gambling regulation that varies by country. Confirm the rules that apply to your own jurisdiction and your target GEOs before running campaigns.
